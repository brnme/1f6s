Prohibited Red Lines

"1 frame / 6 seconds" is not universal. Its essence is "trade time for space", and it only suits content with "low information density and slow visual change". The moment a video's core value depends on "continuous motion", "emotional delivery", or "precise timing", this standard destroys the content itself.

Fatal weakness

The fatal weakness of 1f6s is the lack of motion continuity. The scenarios below are clearly unsuitable — some are strictly forbidden. Placing this list prominently as the initiative's "red-line clause" effectively prevents misuse by newcomers.

I. Physical world & precision motion (picture = evidence / data)

The core of these videos is "recording the process" or "capturing the instant" — a missing frame equals failed evidence.

🚨 Security surveillance / security footage

The goal is to capture the instant a "thief breaks in" or a "person falls abnormally". With only one frame every 6 seconds, a thief can reach the window at second 2 and vanish by second 4 — the footage shows only the window open/closed with the whole process missing, and the footage loses its legal value.

🏃 Sports events / motion-form analysis

Golf swings, 100m sprints, boxing punches — micro-corrections happen in milliseconds. At one frame per 6 seconds you only see "the start" and "the finish line"; the muscle engagement in between is gone, completely useless for training review.

⚙️ Precision manufacturing / machine-operation monitoring

Robotic arms picking on a line, high-speed bearings, chip-placement machines. A 6-second gap is enough for dozens of machine cycles — the compressed video cannot show whether equipment stutters or vibrates.

🔬 Surgery / medical teaching

A surgeon's suturing technique, the advance/retreat of an endoscope. If the "thread-lifting" move at second 3 is skipped, students are baffled: "why did the thread suddenly go through?" — a serious misdirection for teaching.

II. Sensory & artistic expression (picture = emotion / atmosphere)

The essence of these videos is "flowing aesthetics" — sampling siphons away the soul.

🎬 Films / TV dramas / narrative shorts

The nuance of an actor's micro-expressions (sliding from a smile to tears), the trajectory of falling raindrops, the flicker of candlelight. One frame per 6 seconds turns an art film into "PPT paging" — a Wong Kar-wai film becomes a "slideshow of stills", destroying montage and narrative rhythm entirely.

🎵 Music videos (MV) / live concerts

The picture must lock to the musical beat — the drum hit should land exactly on the dancer's explosive move. At one frame per 6 seconds, action and rhythm go wildly out of sync, looking like a buffering disaster.

💃 Dance / movement arts

Ballet grand jetés, hip-hop waving, tango head snaps. Lose the intermediate frames and flowing beauty becomes "a skipping zombie" — entirely losing its watchability.

🏔️ Natural scenery / travel vlogs

Foam hitting the rocks, clouds rolling over mountains, silk-like waterfalls. One frame per 6 seconds turns these gorgeous moving scenes into static postcards, losing the immersive "being there" feeling.

III. Human–computer interaction & teaching demos (picture = operational logic)

These videos are about "mouse trajectories" and "interface feedback" — stuttering makes the operation path incomprehensible.

🖱️ Software tutorials / design demos (e.g. PS, PR, CAD)

Dragging a slider, marquee-selecting layers, stretching a curve. The drag is half-done at second 2 and only refreshed at second 6 — viewers never see the real-time preview changes during the drag and simply cannot learn the technique.

🎮 Esports / gameplay footage (FPS, MOBA)

Aiming, positioning, skill projectile trajectories. One frame per 6 seconds means you only see "I was under tower" and "I'm dead" — the entire getting-ganked process is gone, and fans will roast the creator to trending.

🚁 Remote operation / drone first-person view (FPV)

Micro joystick adjustments for dodging obstacles. Jumpy footage gives the operator motion sickness and makes real spatial distance impossible to judge.

IV. Extreme "brainstorm" & "physically-impossible" content (let imagination fly)

These scenarios look niche, but misuse of 1f6s here is often both absurd and serious.

🤟 Real-time interpreting / sign-language translation videos

Sign language uses continuous hand trajectories and orientation to express grammar and tense. Sampling every 6 seconds shreds sign-language grammar, leaving deaf viewers with "grammatically scrambled gibberish" they cannot understand.

🍳 Cooking tutorials (the pan-toss)

In those two seconds the food flies out of the pan, viewers want the visual temptation of "golden ingredients tumbling through the air". At one frame per 6 seconds you only see "food in the pan" and "food in the plate" — the state-bistro pan-toss virtuosity is erased, and food videos lose their "wok hei".

🐱 Pet / baby behavior observation

The stretch of a kitten's full-body yawn, the trembling muscle effort of a baby's first roll-over. What makes these cute/touching is precisely the detail in continuous frames. At one frame per 6 seconds, even the cutest cat becomes a "staged specimen" and cutesy accounts instantly lose followers.

🧼 ASMR (visual triggers)

Cutting soap, fluid mixing, sand art. These rely on continuous visual white noise to relax the nerves — jumpy images don't lull; they trigger anxiety and discomfort.

Reverse Judgment: one question decides it

Before compressing, every operator should ask themselves one question —

"If you deleted everything in between these 6 seconds and only kept the start and end points, would viewers still find this video 'valuable' or 'professional'?"

If the answer is "yes — it's just a few lines being spoken" → go ahead and use 1f6s.

If the answer is "no — there's craft / beauty / evidence in between" → drop it immediately and use traditional video compression (keep the original frame rate; use CRF only, or drop resolution to 720p).

Where the red-line clause sits

Keeping this as the initiative's "forbidden list (red lines)" in a prominent place effectively stops newcomers from misusing it and protects the delivery quality of core content. 1f6s is a tool, not a faith — knowing when not to use it matters more than knowing when to.
